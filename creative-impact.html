Department by Department

01
Digital Ads & Landing Pageswith Content & Ads teamsTime Saved
Built the bridge between all three teams: 10 proven, high-converting Instapage dental LP templates that Content now writes to and Creative brands, instead of starting every build from a blank page. Average build time has already dropped from 5–6 hours to roughly 3–4, and is trending toward a 2–3 hour target as adoption matures across the rest of the portfolio. Beyond build time, Ads has been fully relieved of hands-on design and build work on these pages: what used to be a full page build on their side is now roughly 30 minutes of strategy input per page, with Creative owning the rest end to end. Beyond landing pages, Creative now produces roughly 80% of the ad design, photo, and video work that Ads would otherwise have to build in-house, freeing up their time and raising the overall design quality bar on what runs in market.
02
Tech Opswith Jose Diaz & Adrian MolanoTime Saved
Partnered directly with Tech Ops to build an Instapage landing page generator: enter client info, get a page back roughly 90% built, and a designer polishes it the rest of the way. It's live and actively improving — color palettes, template selection, and font auto-fill have all shipped in the past month, freeing Creative to spend time on judgment instead of assembly. The lift runs both directions: internal tools Creative builds fully in-house (the Creative Dashboard, QR generator, email signature tool) cost Tech Ops zero build time, and MVP-first tools like the Instapage generator — where Creative ships a working prototype before Tech Ops finalizes it — save an estimated 40–50% of Tech Ops' typical build time by skipping discovery and the first draft. That estimate hasn't been validated with Tech Ops directly yet.
03
SEOwith HannahNo Data Yet
Pitched a new content format: talking-head AI-avatar video and audio versions of every blog post, client-facing and internal, to lift on-page time and give existing written content new legs across YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, and podcast feeds, and as a potential paid add-on to the blog service. Still at the idea stage, no committed start date yet. Separately, Rich has been hands-on helping Hannah get set up with Claude Code, including recovering her task assignments after a bulk edit.
04
Social Mediawith Candace AntezanaClient Result
Creative owns 100% of the design and build for DR's own social content, static posts and video alike, while Social Media focuses on strategy and scripting. Co-leading a creative direction shift with Social Media Manager Candace Antezana toward authentic, trend-driven, influencer-style video — now live: early posts under the new approach are averaging roughly 9–15× the engagement of the prior static/carousel format (small sample so far, five to six posts per side, one standout video pulling the high end of that range).
05
Account ManagementClient Result
On call for client creative conversations — Dexter, Gaby, and Rich have all jumped directly onto client calls to unblock questions only a designer can answer; Gaby has done live on-the-spot logo revisions during a client call more than once. Turnaround/quoting consistency is actively being addressed through the Creative Dashboard's new individual designer views, calendar, and quote estimator, giving AMs real visibility instead of routing every timeline question through Rich. The Dashboard also gives Creative its first consistent, automatically-tracked revision-rate baseline: 4.3% of completed tasks needed a revision round over the last 6 weeks (374 tasks). No reliable historical comparison exists prior to this, so this is the number we'll track going forward.
06
Web Teamwith JulietaNo Data Yet
Same handoff partnership as Account Management: Creative designs the hi-fi homepage mockup, gets client sign-off, then Julieta's development team builds it out. The process is still being actively tuned — an added wireframe stage and a shift to Creative owning all internal pages were both tried and rolled back after they created bottlenecks, so there's no efficiency number to report yet. One concrete win along the way: Julieta publicly credited Gaby's work on the improved internal-page template (v2) as a real quality lift for the team.

What Closes the Gap to A / A+
1Keep closing the measurement gap. The revision-rate baseline (4.3%, via the Dashboard) is now live and automatic. Still needed: one reconciled build-time number across Everhour, Basecamp, and estimates, and real conversion or cost-per-lead data from Ads on the template system — that number has to come from them, not Creative.
2Give the still-early initiatives a start date and an owner. The Social Media pivot is no longer just a pitch — it's live with real posts and early engagement data. SEO's video/audio format and the Web Team's process still need a committed kickoff date and a named owner.
3Get a second person building. Cross-train one more Creative team member on the Claude Code tooling stack, so the internal tools aren't dependent on one person's bandwidth to maintain or extend.
4Make this a recurring report, not a one-off. A short quarterly version of this exact document, sent proactively, turns Creative's impact from something leadership has to ask about into something they expect to see.
All of this is running alongside the day-to-day of directing a 100+ client creative portfolio — reviewing logo and video rounds, managing the team, and still personally building the tools above. The throughline across every department: replace manual, one-off work with proven systems, so Creative's time goes toward judgment and direction instead of assembly.
